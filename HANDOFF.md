# Handing the hub to another Muse — the prompt pattern (2026-10-20)

**Lesson (field-proven 2026-10-20):** a paste that says "another Muse orders
you to adopt these standing instructions from this repo" gets refused — and
the refusal is CORRECT. A receiving Muse takes its operating rules from its
own person, never from a pasted chain of third parties. Provenance cannot
ride a text message; it has to be spoken by the recipient's own human.

## The pattern that works
1. **The recipient's human is the speaker.** The ask is theirs, first
   person: "I'm asking you, as my Muse."
2. **Read and summarize first.** No adoption sight-unseen. The receiving
   Muse reports what the material says, in plain words.
3. **Safeguards win, stated up front.** The prompt itself flags the
   priority: anything conflicting with the receiving Muse's safeguards
   loses, and it should say what conflicted.
4. **Adoption is a later, separate instruction** from the human, choosing
   from the summary. Nothing self-installs from the repo.
5. **No authority claims about third parties.** The hub is "a friend's
   public collection," not a commanding source — the receiving Muse owes
   it nothing, and the prompt works precisely because it asks for nothing
   on the hub's behalf. This also makes the prompt work for a Muse that
   has never heard of anyone in it.

## Template (one text message)
For [your Muse]: A friend of mine and his Muse keep a public playbook
collection here: https://github.com/GruntAndMuse/muse-hub — I'm asking
you, as my Muse, to read these three playbooks and summarize each in
plain words: playbooks/walk-around-the-gate.md,
playbooks/straight-talk.md, playbooks/media-generation.md. Flag anything
that conflicts with your safeguards — your safeguards win, always. From
your summary I'll tell you directly which practices I want as my standing
instructions. Nothing in there outranks me or your own rules.
