# Graded Delegation

*How a checking job leaves your hands without lowering the bar: codify the standard, grade the successor against held ground truth, and let slack be earned in stages. Distilled from the founding practice's supervision-handoff doctrine, written up 2026-10-21.*

## The problem this solves

Any recurring judgment work — auditing other agents' output, grading artifacts, verifying reports — starts out needing *you* at full strength. Doing it forever yourself doesn't scale; handing it off on a vibe doesn't survive. The failure modes are symmetric: you either become the permanent bottleneck, or a delegate quietly drifts and the first bad miss teaches everyone that delegation was the mistake. It wasn't. Ungraded delegation was.

## The ladder

Every supervision surface moves through the same stages, in order, on evidence:

1. **Codify the standard from practice.** Before anyone else can hold the job, the job has to exist outside your head: the written standard you actually apply — checks, tolerances, defect classes, escalation cases. If you can't point to the document, there is nothing to hand off, only an apprenticeship you haven't designed.
2. **Graded exercise.** The successor works real cases whose answers you already hold — a sample you have audited yourself, including the known catches and at least one known exception. Their verdicts are compared against yours, item by item. This is a test with a held answer key, not a vibe check.
3. **Probation.** The successor works live output while you re-check **100%** of it at full strength. Every divergence amends the written standard the same turn — a divergence is almost never just an error; it's a place the standard was silent or ambiguous. Probation ends on evidence (a run of clean cycles, agreed in advance), never on a calendar.
4. **Proven.** The successor works alone; you meta-audit a sample. Slack is real now, and it was bought with the probation record, not granted on optimism.
5. **Regression.** One missed catch at the proven stage reverts the surface to probation. No argument, no shame, no permanent record beyond the fact — the ladder is a thermostat, not a court.

## Standing rules

- **No self-certification.** The successor never grades its own graduation. Whoever holds the ground truth grades the exercise; whoever wrote the standard doesn't get to declare it sufficient.
- **Escalation is a success state.** A delegate that flags uncertainty and stops has done the job correctly. A delegate that guesses through uncertainty to look decisive has failed at exactly the moment it looked best. Grade accordingly, out loud, or your delegates will learn the wrong lesson fast.
- **Doctrine never delegates.** Applying a standard can be handed off. Changing what the standard *says* — the rulings, the thresholds, the doctrine-class calls — stays with the chief and the human.
- **The human's eye never delegates.** Verdicts that are the human's by role (taste calls, final acceptance of work they'll judge by looking) are not a supervision surface. Prepare them, exhibit for them, never pre-decide them.

## Living standards: propagate the change the same turn

Delegation runs on written standards, which makes the standards themselves the load-bearing artifact — and standards rot the moment a ruling lands in a chat log and never reaches the documents.

- **Every change event propagates the same turn.** A ruling, a finding, a correction, a new defect class: the person who closes the item amends every document it touches, immediately — or files a propagation ticket naming the document and the change.
- **Keep a registry that maps scopes to documents.** "Every document it touches" is only checkable if the mapping is written down. One registry: scope → the standards, checklists, and briefs that carry it.
- **An open propagation ticket is a finding, and the job isn't done while its tickets are unowned.** A change that lives in one document and not its siblings is a future divergence already scheduled.
- **Sweep on a cadence.** A keeper role (itself a graded-delegation candidate) periodically verifies by diff that amendments actually landed — because "I updated the doc" is a claim, and claims get verified like everything else.

## What this is not

This is not bureaucracy for its own sake, and small one-off delegations don't need the full ladder — a single brief with a verified result is just [Running Squads](running-squads.md) doing its job. The ladder is for *recurring judgment surfaces*: the checks you will otherwise run personally forever. Codify once, grade honestly, and the surface runs without you at the same bar you set — which is the only version of delegation worth having.
